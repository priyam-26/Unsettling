04 / THE QUESTIONS

Why was she there?

Why was she alone?

Why did she trust someone?

Why did she go?

Why did she come home late?

Why did she...

Stop.

Every question has quietly moved the burden from the person who chose to harm her
to the person who was harmed.

06 / THE BURDEN WAS NEVER HERS

So let us stop asking what she should have done differently.

Nothing.

Not the clothes.
Not the hour.
Not the road.
Not the dream.
Not the trust.
Not the fact that she was there.

A woman can make a mistake. She can be careless. She can trust the wrong person. She can walk into the wrong place at the wrong time. None of it gives another human being permission to violate her.

07 / THE WORLD KEEPS GOING

The case may continue.

It is not the end of everything that was lost.

The file moves. Hearings happen. Evidence is recorded. Orders are passed. Appeals are filed.

Then the court closes its file for the day, the headline moves down the page, and another morning arrives.

For some people, time keeps moving.
For others, it moves around an absence.

08 / WHAT A NUMBER CANNOT HOLD

A case can count what the law needs to count.

But these are the things a number cannot carry.

the conversations that never happened the plans that stayed unfinished the version of herself she never got to become the careers never started the songs never sung the friendships never made the silence at a table where someone used to sit the people who still remember her laugh

You can count a case. You can count a sentence. You can count the years. You cannot count a life that never got the chance to finish becoming itself.

09 / THE NUMBER KEEPS MOVING

In 2024, India recorded
29,536 rape cases.

Yet the numbers keep coming.

That is an average of about 81 reported cases every day.
Spread evenly across the year, that is roughly one reported case every 17 minutes 47 seconds. [source]

And since 2010, the headlines have carried names and cases like these — selected, not complete. A year can hold far more than one story.

These are not the whole story.

They are only the stories that became impossible for the country to ignore.

So perhaps the question was never “why didn't she...?”
Perhaps we should ask
why did someone think they "could?"

The numbers on this page are reported cases, not a measure of every incident that occurred. The 17-minute figure is a mathematical average, not a literal schedule of crimes. The 2024 figure comes from the NCRB's Crime in India 2024 release.
